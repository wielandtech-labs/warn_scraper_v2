## Sentiment

Job losses reported in WARN notices across Florida eased sharply to 2,634 in the current 90-day window, down 70.5% from 8,934 in the prior window and 42.7% below the 4,598 recorded in the same window last year. Recent weeks may rise as late filings arrive. The long-run picture agrees: trailing-12-month job losses of 19,263 are 33.9% below the 29,133 of the twelve months before.

The relief is concentrated in South Florida, where Broward eased 90.2% to 365 and Miami-Dade eased 92.5% to 120. Elsewhere pressure rose: St. Lucie recorded 494 job losses, up from 51, and Okaloosa climbed to 309 from 53.

By sector, Transportation & Warehousing still accounts for the most job losses at 557, though that is down 88.6% from 4,906. Manufacturing moved the other way, rising 85.3% to 491, while Administrative, Support & Waste Management eased 89.6% to 70.

The model projects 1,814 job losses for 2026-10 (range 284–3,345) and 1,429 for 2027-03 (range 0–2,988).

## Analysis

All three comparisons point toward relief, and the decline against the prior window is steep. Part of that reflects an unusually heavy prior window: 2026-05 recorded 5,374 job losses against 1,799 a year earlier. From 2026-07 through 2026-09 each month came in below the same month last year, for example 423 in 2026-09 against 2,589.

Geography has shifted away from the largest metro counties toward mid-sized ones. St. Lucie, Okaloosa, Brevard and Polk all worsened from small prior-window bases. The county field also mixes labels such as "Broward" and "Broward County", so those rows should be read together.

The 2026-10 row is partial, so its 30 job losses are not comparable with the 1,529 recorded in the same month a year earlier.
