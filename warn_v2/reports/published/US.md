## Sentiment

Job losses reported in WARN notices nationwide eased to 58,032 in the current 90-day window, down 47.5% from 110,441 in the prior window and 36.6% below the 91,472 recorded in the same window last year. Both comparisons point the same way, and the long-run picture agrees: trailing-12-month job losses of 363,052 sit 12.8% below the 416,157 of the twelve months before.

The relief is not evenly shared. Washington is the clear exception, with job losses climbing to 16,724 from 4,673 (257.9%), more than any other state this window. Ohio (3,300, up 22.1%) and Georgia (2,821, up 18.2%) also worsened, while Florida eased sharply to 2,634 from 8,934.

By sector, Manufacturing still accounts for the most job losses at 10,093, though that is down 44.4%. Health Care & Social Assistance moved the other way, rising 29.4% to 5,861, and Agriculture, Forestry, Fishing & Hunting jumped to 4,840 from 97. Administrative, Support & Waste Management eased the most, down 84.0%.

BLS reports total nonfarm payrolls changed by +29 thousand in 2026-09, with the unemployment rate at 4.2%. The model projects 31,185 job losses for 2026-10 (range 18,653–43,717) and 27,026 for 2027-03 (range 12,842–41,211).

## Analysis

The headline decline is broad: momentum, the seasonal comparison and the 12-month trend all show easing. It should still be read with two data caveats. First, states post WARN notices with a lag, so the most recent weeks of the current window are likely to be revised upward as late filings arrive. Second, the 2026-10 row covers only the first days of the month, so its 2,169 job losses are not comparable with the 53,894 recorded in 2025-10.

Concentration is the bigger story. Washington alone accounts for 16,724 of the 58,032 national job losses this window, at a time when most large states eased. Sector rows cover 84.7% of current-window notices, so the industry picture is well grounded.
