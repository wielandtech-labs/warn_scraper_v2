## Sentiment

Job losses in Texas WARN notices eased to 4,248 in the current 90-day window, down 33.2% from 6,356 in the prior window and 31.6% below the 6,212 recorded in the same window last year. Both comparisons point the same way, while the long run is close to flat: trailing-12-month job losses of 23,627 are 1.5% below the 23,981 of the twelve months before.

Dallas is the exception, with losses climbing 74.4% to 2,208 from 1,266, and Denton worsened to 327 from 90 (263.3%). Harris eased sharply, to 121 from 1,600.

By sector, Transportation & Warehousing leads at 638, up 67.0%, and Health Care & Social Assistance recorded 307 where no comparable activity was recorded in the prior window. Wholesale Trade eased the most, down 83.2% to 347 from 2,064.

The model projects 1,226 job losses for 2026-10 (range 0–2,891) and 2,750 for 2027-03 (range 1,032–4,469).

## Analysis

The statewide easing masks concentration: Dallas accounts for 2,208 of the 4,248 job losses this window, at a time when Harris, Bexar and Tarrant all eased. Notice counts are also lower than a year ago, at 33 against 81 in the same window last year.

The monthly path is mixed. 2026-09 recorded 2,448 job losses against 2,068 a year earlier, a worsening, while 2026-07 (1,180 against 3,722) was well below. The 2026-10 row covers only the first days of the month, so its 28 job losses are partial and not comparable with last year. States post WARN notices with a lag, so recent weeks may rise as late filings arrive.

Sector rows cover 69.7% of current-window notices, so the industry picture is reasonably grounded but incomplete.
