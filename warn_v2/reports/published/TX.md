## Sentiment

Texas job losses worsened to 3,718 in the current 90-day window from 2,732 in the prior window, a 36.1% rise, but fell 20.5% from 4,677 in the same window last year. Momentum and the seasonal comparison point in different directions. Trailing-12-month job losses of 22,239 are 6.8% below the 23,872 of the prior 12 months.

Geographically, Dallas County leads with 1,648 job losses, up 35.0% from 1,221. Bexar (579) and Tarrant (494) counties recorded losses where no comparable activity was recorded in the prior window, while Harris County eased 72.9% to 121 from 447.

By sector, Transportation & Warehousing worsened 141.3% to 567 from 235, Health Care & Social Assistance recorded 325 after no comparable activity, and Wholesale Trade rose 368.9% to 286 from 61. Manufacturing eased 68.0% to 123 from 384.

The model projects 2,078 job losses for 2026-11 (range 402–3,753) and 2,206 for 2027-01 (range 509–3,903).

## Analysis

The near-term picture has worsened while the year-on-year view still shows relief. The rise is concentrated in Dallas, Bexar and Tarrant counties, which together account for most of the current window.

Notice counts tell a different long-run story from worker counts: trailing-12-month notices of 140 are far below the 294 of the prior 12 months, while job losses fell only 6.8%, so the average notice is larger.

The monthly path is uneven: 2026-09 at 2,448 was above its year-earlier 2,068, while 2026-07 at 1,180 was well below 3,722. The 2026-10 row is partial.
