## Sentiment

Job losses in Retail Trade eased to 3,474 in the current 90-day window, down 26.1% from 4,703 in the prior window and 29.8% below the 4,948 recorded in the same window last year. The long-run picture points the other way: trailing-12-month job losses of 40,489 are 7.1% above the 37,802 of the twelve months before, which keeps the sector at Grade C.

Geographically, New Jersey worsened most, with job losses climbing 846.1% to 1,088 from 115. Pennsylvania rose 813.7% to 466, and Washington rose 236.8% to 357. South Carolina eased 83.0% to 212 from 1,245.

By subsector, Food and Beverage Retailers worsened, up 109.8% to 1,397, and Motor Vehicle and Parts Dealers climbed to 702 from 53. Furniture, Home Furnishings, Electronics, and Appliance Retailers eased 99.4% to 11 from 1,834.

BLS reports Retail trade payrolls fell by 5.1 thousand in 2026-08 and rose by 5.8 thousand in 2026-09. These WARN figures cover only NAICS-enriched notices and are directional.

## Analysis

The divergence between the 90-day and 12-month views is the key feature. The trailing total is lifted by a single heavy month, 2026-01, with 16,511 job losses against 5,452 a year earlier; most months since have run near or below their year-earlier levels. The 2026-10 row is partial, so its 210 is not comparable with the 6,203 of a year earlier.

Within the easing window, pressure is shifting between states and subsectors rather than fading evenly: Food and Beverage Retailers and Motor Vehicle and Parts Dealers both rose sharply while furniture and electronics retailers nearly cleared.
